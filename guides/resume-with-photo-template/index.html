How to use a resume template with a photo

When a photo belongs on a resume, how to place a professional picture in a Word or Google Docs template, and when a plain layout is the better choice.

Some resume templates include a space for a photograph. Whether you should use that space depends on the job, the employer and the country you are applying in, not on how the template looks. This guide explains when a photo is appropriate, how to place one in a Word or Google Docs template, and when to switch to a layout without a picture. For general editing, proofreading and saving a final PDF, use How to edit a resume and cover letter template and save a final PDF.

1. Decide whether this application should include a photo

In the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and similar hiring contexts, a photo is usually not expected on a resume for most office, internship and professional jobs. Many career offices advise leaving it off. A picture is rarely needed to judge your work, and it can introduce bias that is not relevant to the role. Some application systems also extract text more reliably from a page that is text first.

A photo can be appropriate when:

  • The job posting or the employer clearly asks for one
  • You are applying in a country or field where a photo CV is still customary. This remains common in parts of Europe, and for some public-facing, performing or on-camera work
  • A portfolio or casting brief specifically calls for a headshot

If the posting does not mention a photo and you are applying in the United States, a resume without a picture is the safer default. When you are unsure, follow the employer's instructions, or ask a career office or the recruiter before you send a photo.

2. If you need a photo, start with a simple headshot

Use a recent photo, taken in clear light, with a plain background. Dress the way you would for that workplace. Your face should be easy to see, and the crop should be tight enough that the picture does not take over the page. Avoid a heavy filter, a group shot, or a low-resolution crop from a social post. The goal is a professional likeness, not a decorative portrait.

3. Place the photo in the template without crowding the text

SpoonfulOfCalmStudio's photo resume templates are made for Word and Google Docs, and they include a photo placeholder. Many also include a matching cover letter. The 10 Photo Resume Templates with Cover Letters and the 50 Photo Resume Templates with Cover Letters are bundles of those layouts.

Open a copy of the template, click the placeholder, and insert your picture. If the image looks stretched, crop it to the frame rather than dragging only one side. Keep your name, contact details and section headings easy to read. If the photo crowds the text, make the picture slightly smaller or adjust the margin around it until the page looks balanced. If a template font is not installed on your computer, choose one clear font and use it throughout, as described in the editing guide.

4. When to hide the photo or use a different template

If you are applying in the United States, or to an employer that asked for no photo, use a template that was designed without one. You can also delete or hide the picture placeholder in a photo template, then close up the space so the page does not look unfinished. Student and entry-level designs without a photo, such as the First Internship Student Resume Template and the Scholar Student Resume Template, are a straightforward option. Browse all resume templates if you want to compare layouts.

College students writing an internship resume can also follow How to write a college student resume for an internship.

5. Check how the file will be read, then save a PDF

Some employers use software that reads a resume automatically. A simple layout with standard section headings is generally easier for those systems to parse. A photo does not help that process and can get in the way. No template can guarantee how a particular system will handle your file.

Unless the application asks for a Word document, save a final PDF so the layout and the photo stay in place. Open the PDF on your computer and confirm that the picture is sharp, that it is not cut off, and that your contact details are still clear.

6. A short note on privacy

Only include a photo when you are comfortable sharing it with that employer. You do not need to put a full mailing address on the resume; city and region are usually enough.

A note on results

A well-chosen layout helps your materials look consistent. It cannot guarantee interviews or job offers, and a photo will not make up for unclear content. Match the template to the employer's expectations first, then spend your time on the work itself.
