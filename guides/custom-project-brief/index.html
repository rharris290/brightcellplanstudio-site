How to write a brief for a custom CRM, app or website project

What to include when you email about a custom CRM, app or website, with a fill-in brief template.

Ricardo takes on custom projects, including custom CRM software for small businesses, custom app development, and website design and development. None of these is a ready-made product sold on this site. Each one is scoped individually, starting with a conversation by email. A clear brief helps you get a useful answer faster and helps both sides agree on what's being built.

1. Describe the problem, not just the solution

Start with one or two sentences about what isn't working today. For example:

  • "We track leads in three spreadsheets and keep missing follow-ups."
  • "Customers book by phone and text, and we double-book."
  • "We need a simple site that explains our services and gets enquiries."

The problem tells us what success looks like.

2. Say who will use it

  • Just you, your team, or your customers?
  • Roughly how many people?
  • Will they use it on a computer, a phone, or both?

3. List your must-haves

Write the three to five things it absolutely has to do. For a CRM, that might be storing contacts, logging calls and notes, tracking deals by stage, and reminding you to follow up. For a website, it might be a services page, a contact form and a gallery. Keep this list short. A focused first version is easier to scope.

4. List nice-to-haves separately

Extras such as reports, integrations or a customer portal can be planned for later. Keeping them separate makes it clear what the first version needs.

5. Share examples

Links to tools, apps or websites you like, and a note on what you like about each, are more useful than long descriptions. Tell us what you dislike in tools you've already tried, too.

6. Describe your current data

  • Where does your information live now: spreadsheets, paper, email, another tool?
  • Roughly how much is there?
  • Will it need to move into the new system?

7. Answer the practical questions

These shape the scope, and each is agreed in writing. None is assumed to be included:

  • Accounts and logins: do people need to sign in? Different access levels?
  • Payments: will it take payments or subscriptions?
  • Domain and hosting: do you already have a domain or a hosting account? Who will own and pay for them?
  • Privacy: will it store personal or sensitive information?
  • Updates and maintenance: who will make changes after launch?
  • Content: who will provide the text, photos and logo?

8. Share timing and budget

Give any real deadline, such as a launch or a season, and a rough budget range if you have one. This helps shape a realistic first version. Price and timing are always confirmed with you before work begins.

9. Send it

Email brightcellplanners@gmail.com with the subject Custom project. Bullet points are fine. Consultations happen by email, and you'll get an honest answer about whether the project is a fit and what's possible. Not every request will be.
