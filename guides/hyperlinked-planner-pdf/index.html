How to choose and open a hyperlinked PDF planner on your tablet

Check compatibility, save your file, open it in a note-taking app, test the links and back up your planner.

A hyperlinked digital planner is a PDF with built-in tabs and buttons that jump between pages. It works much like a paper planner you write in with a stylus, but a tap takes you to any month, week or section. This guide covers choosing a planner that suits your device and getting it open in a note-taking app. If you use GoodNotes on iPad, our separate guide, How to use a digital planner in GoodNotes, goes into that app in more detail.

1. Check the listing before you buy

Every planner listing names the apps or devices it was designed for. That's the most reliable guide. Look for:

  • The app names in the title or description, such as GoodNotes or other PDF annotation apps
  • Whether it's made for an e-ink tablet. E-ink planners are laid out for black-and-white screens. A colourful iPad planner may not look the same on an e-ink device.
  • Dated or undated. See our guide to choosing between them.
  • What's included, such as sections, page styles or bonus stickers

If your app or device isn't mentioned, email brightcellplanners@gmail.com before buying and ask. Not every planner behaves the same way in every app.

2. Save the file somewhere you can find it

Website orders arrive by email, usually within 24 hours of payment. Etsy orders appear under Purchases in your Etsy account. Open the file on your tablet and save it somewhere easy to reach, such as your tablet's files app or a cloud folder you already use. Keep an untouched copy as a backup, so you can always start fresh.

Some planners come as a compressed (zip) file. Most tablets can open these from the files app, which unpacks the PDF inside.

3. Open the PDF in your note-taking app

The exact steps differ from app to app, and apps change their menus over time, so check your app's own help pages for current instructions. The general approach is usually one of these:

  • Import from inside the app. Look for an option to import or add a PDF, then choose your planner file.
  • Share it to the app. In your files app, open the PDF and use the share option to send it to your note-taking app.

Import the PDF as a single document. Don't split it into separate pages, or the links between pages may stop working.

4. Test the links before you write

Before writing anything, tap a few tabs and buttons to make sure they jump where you expect. Many note-taking apps have two modes: one for writing and one for reading or tapping links. If tapping draws a line instead of following a link, switch to the app's read, view or hand/select mode and try again.

5. Make it yours

  • Pick one or two pens and a highlighter so planning stays quick.
  • Add digital stickers if your app supports images. BrightCellPlanStudio sells pre-cropped PNG sticker sets.
  • Fill in key dates such as birthdays, bills and appointments first.

6. Back up your planner regularly

Your handwriting lives inside your note-taking app, so use the app's backup or export option regularly, or keep a copy in cloud storage. If you change tablets, you can then move your filled-in planner across.

Troubleshooting

  • Links don't respond: check you're not in a drawing mode, and that the PDF was imported as one document.
  • The page looks cut off or tiny: try your app's zoom or fit-to-screen setting.
  • The file won't open: make sure it isn't still compressed, then try importing again.
