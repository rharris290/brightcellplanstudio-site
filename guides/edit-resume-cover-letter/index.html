How to edit a resume and cover letter template and save a final PDF

Fill in a Word or Google Docs template, tailor your cover letter, proofread and save a clean PDF.

SpoonfulOfCalmStudio's resume templates are made for Word and Google Docs, and many come with a matching cover letter. This guide walks through making a template your own and preparing a clean file to send to employers.

1. Open and save a copy

Open the template in the program named on the listing, Word or Google Docs. In Google Docs, make your own copy so you can edit it. Save the copy with a clear name, such as Firstname-Lastname-Resume. Keep the original as a blank backup.

2. Gather your information first

Before typing, collect:

  • Contact details: name, phone, email, city, and a professional profile link if you have one
  • Jobs, with titles, employers, dates and key responsibilities
  • Education and certifications
  • Skills that match the jobs you're applying for

3. Replace placeholder text section by section

Work top to bottom. Click into each placeholder and type over it, so the formatting stays in place. If you paste from elsewhere, use your program's paste-without-formatting option, so fonts and sizes stay consistent.

4. Write strong bullet points

  • Start with an action word: managed, built, organised, improved.
  • Focus on results and responsibilities, and include numbers when you can.
  • Keep each bullet to one or two lines.
  • List your most recent role first.

5. Adjust the layout carefully

  • Remove sections you don't need rather than leaving them empty.
  • Keep it to one or two pages, depending on your experience.
  • Change colours or fonts sparingly. Templates are designed to balance, so small changes usually work best.
  • If a template font isn't installed, your program may swap it. Pick one clear font and use it throughout.

6. Tailor your cover letter

Use the matching cover letter template so your application looks consistent. For each job:

  • Address the hiring manager by name if you can find it
  • Explain why you want this role at this company
  • Connect two or three of your achievements to what the job asks for
  • Close with a short, polite call to action

7. Proofread

Read it aloud, check every date and contact detail, and ask someone else to look it over. Small typos are the easiest thing to fix.

8. Save the final version as a PDF

Most employers accept PDFs, and a PDF keeps your layout fixed on any device. In Word, use Save As or Export and choose PDF. In Google Docs, use the File menu's option to save as a PDF document. Open the PDF afterwards to check that nothing has shifted.

Some job portals ask for a Word file instead, so always follow the application instructions. Some employers use applicant tracking systems that read resumes automatically. A simple, clearly labelled layout with standard section headings is generally easier to read, but no template can guarantee how a particular system will handle it.

A note on results

A well-presented resume helps your experience get noticed, but it can't guarantee interviews or job offers. Your content and how well it matches each role matter most.
