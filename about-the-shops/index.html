BrightCellPlanStudio: spreadsheets, digital planners and presentation templates

BrightCellPlanStudio is for people who like to plan. That covers budgets and bills, weekly schedules and long-term goals, a small business or a pitch deck. The shop is built around four product families, and each one is made for a different way of working.

Excel and Google Sheets spreadsheet templates

Browse: Spreadsheets

Our spreadsheet templates are for people who would rather type than handwrite and want the math done for them. The range includes:

A tip for choosing a budget template

Start smaller than you think you need. A simple monthly budget you fill in every week will teach you more than a 20-tab bundle you open once. When the habit sticks, move up to an annual budget or an all-in-one personal finance bundle.

Hyperlinked digital planners

Browse: Digital Planners

A digital planner is a hyperlinked PDF that you write in with a stylus on a tablet. Tabs and buttons take you to a month, week, day or section in one tap instead of scrolling through hundreds of pages. You get the feel of a paper planner with everything in one device.

BrightCellPlanStudio has the largest range on this site, covering:

  • Life and all-in-one planners for running a busy week in one place
  • Budget and finance planners for people who prefer to handwrite their money plans, including cash stuffing planners
  • Health and wellness journals for tracking symptoms, medication, blood pressure, pain, moods, anxiety and appointments
  • Kids and family planners, from baby memory books to back-to-school planners and family organisers
  • Wedding and event planners for weddings, bachelorettes, baby showers and holidays
  • Faith planners, including Christian planners, Bible study journals and Bible reading plans
  • Hobby journals for baking, coffee, books, art, gardening, camping, bird watching, aquariums and more
  • Business planners for content creators, bloggers, cleaning businesses and other small business owners
  • Vintage and themed planners for people who want a particular look

Dated or undated?

  • Dated planners come with the calendar for a specific year, such as 2027, already filled in. Open the planner and start writing.
  • Undated planners have blank dates. You can start in any month, and if you skip a few weeks, no pages go to waste.

If you tend to start strong in January and drift off by spring, an undated planner is often the kinder choice.

Ready-made template or custom project?

Ready-made products are the quickest and most affordable route when your need is common: a monthly budget, a weekly planner, a pitch deck, a stream overlay or a resume. Browse the two shops first, because there's a good chance something close already exists.

A custom project makes sense when:

  • You've tried templates and keep changing them to fit how you work
  • Your business has its own steps, terms or records that generic tools don't cover
  • Several people need to use the same system
  • You need a tool that runs in a browser or on a phone, not just in a file
  • You want your own branding throughout

If you're not sure which route fits, describe the problem in an email and ask. Sometimes a small change to an existing template is enough. Sometimes a custom spreadsheet, CRM, app or website is the better long-term answer.

How to write a useful custom project brief

The more you share in your first email, the faster you'll get a clear answer. Email brightcellplanners@gmail.com and include whatever you can from this list:

  1. What it's for. The problem you want solved, in a sentence or two.
  2. Who will use it. You alone, your family, your staff or your customers.
  3. Must-haves. The three to five things it absolutely has to do.
  4. Nice-to-haves. Extras that would be great but aren't essential.
  5. Examples. Links to products, sites or apps you like, and what you like about them.
  6. Where it will be used. Excel, Google Sheets, a tablet, print, a browser or a phone.
  7. Timing. Any date it's needed by.
  8. Budget. A rough range, if you have one.
  9. For software, apps and websites: whether you need user accounts, payments, a domain, hosting, or ongoing updates and maintenance. These questions shape the scope, and each one is agreed in writing.

Consultations happen by email. Details, timing and pricing are always confirmed with you before work starts.

Planning tips that make any template stick

A good template helps, but habits do most of the work. These simple ideas help whichever product you choose.

  • Pick one main tool. A budget spreadsheet for money and a digital planner for time is a common pairing. Spreading the same plans across five tools usually means none get updated.
  • Schedule a weekly check-in. Fifteen minutes every Sunday to review last week and plan the next one is often enough to keep a planner or budget current.
  • Track actual numbers, not just plans. For budgets, the gap between what you planned and what you spent is where the useful lessons are.
  • Keep it light. Use only the sections you need. You can always add more later, and a tidy planner is easier to come back to.
  • Make it yours. Stickers, colours and your own category names make a planner feel personal, and people tend to keep using things they enjoy.
  • Start where you are. If you fall behind, skip to today rather than trying to catch up. Undated planners make this especially easy.

Getting started after you buy

Once your files arrive, by email from a website order or under Purchases on Etsy, these steps help you get going quickly.

Spreadsheets

  1. Open the file in the program named on the product page, Excel or Google Sheets.
  2. If you're using Google Sheets, make your own copy first so your changes are saved to your account.
  3. Start with the settings or instructions tab, if there is one, then enter your own categories and numbers.
  4. Set a weekly reminder to update it. A spreadsheet is only as useful as the numbers in it.

Digital planners

  1. Import the PDF into the note-taking app named on the product page.
  2. Try the tabs and links first so you know how to move between sections.
  3. Pick a pen, a highlighter and a few stickers you'll use regularly, so planning stays quick.
  4. For dated planners, jump straight to today. For undated planners, fill in your starting month.

Presentation templates

  1. Open the deck in PowerPoint or Google Slides, as the listing says.
  2. Delete the layouts you won't use, so the deck stays focused.
  3. Swap in your own colours, fonts, logo and images, then replace placeholder text section by section.

Stream overlays

  1. Unpack the files into a folder you'll keep, so OBS Studio can always find them.
  2. Add each overlay as a source in the right scene. Our OBS guide shows how.
  3. Test your scenes before going live, especially webcam frames and alerts.

Resumes and printables

  1. Open a resume template in Word or Google Docs, as the listing says, and save a copy before editing.
  2. For printables, use your printer's "actual size" or "fit to page" setting and do a test print on one page.

If anything doesn't open or look right, email brightcellplanners@gmail.com with your order details and we'll help.
